## Q1. SOX/SOXX, NVDA and TSM peaks, levels and drawdowns

### Takeaway
The SOX made its record close of **14,634.72 on June 22, 2026** (intraday about 14,655). It entered a bear market in mid-July, recovered partly in August–October, then fell sharply in Oct–Nov. It closed at 10,268.65 on Nov 4 (about −30% from the peak), before falling roughly 9–12% more on Nov 5. **SOXX** closed at **$149.30 on Nov 13** (split-adjusted). That is about 32% below its 52-week high of about $218.4–218.65 (≈$655.95 before the 3:1 split), yet still **+54.67% YTD (NAV total return, as of Nov 12)**. **NVDA** peaked later than the SOX, at a record close of **$239.24 on Oct 6**, and closed at **$162.85 on Nov 13** (−31.9%). **TSM** (ADR) has held up much better: **$398.73 on Nov 13**, −15.7% from an Oct 2 high.

## Q2. What drove the H1 2026 rally (including agentic AI / Claude Code)

### Takeaway
Credible outlets mainly credit the H1 rally to an **AI-driven memory shortage and pricing boom** (DRAM/NAND/HBM), to **semi equipment**, and to a **broadening from GPUs to CPUs and "classic" hardware** under an "agentic AI" narrative. The SOX had its best quarter on record in Q2. Agentic AI was explicitly tied to **CPU and memory demand** (CNBC, Bank of America). Claude Code was cited mainly by analysts and commentary, not by tier-1 outlets, as an inflection point for agents.

### Cited Findings
- The rally was driven mostly by AI demand for memory: DRAM and NAND supply is relatively fixed, so prices rose sharply. In Q2, Intel rose 216.4%, Applied Materials, KLA and Lam more than doubled, NVDA rose 14.7% and AVGO rose 22% — [Axios, Jul 1 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/01/semiconductor-ai-stocks-chips).
- CNBC reported that the new phase of "agentic AI" is driving demand for CPUs and memory and reviving "classic" tech names such as Dell and Intel. AMD soared 74% in April and 46% in May — [CNBC, Jun 1 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/01/how-agentic-ai-is-reviving-classic-tech-stocks-like-dell-and-intel-.html).
- Bank of America said agentic AI could open a market worth more than $170B for server CPUs by 2030. Nvidia, AMD, Intel and Arm rallied on the call — [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/8912253/nvidia-amd-intel-lead-chip-stocks-rally-as-bofa-calls-agentic-ai-gamechanger) (secondary, date unclear).
- SemiAnalysis called Claude Code "the inflection point in Agentic AI". Motley Fool argued that agentic AI needs more CPU compute, which favors Arm — [Motley Fool, Feb 6 2026](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/02/06/anthropics-claude-code-is-taking-over-and-this-ai/) (commentary). Axios noted that Claude Code had become an obsession among VCs and engineers as the foundation of "AI-native and agentic startups" — [Axios AI+ newsletter](https://www.axios.com/newsletters/axios-ai-plus-4ac15800-1273-11f1-b4f4-1b4aa43be5f3.html?stream=top).
- Morgan Stanley reportedly cautioned against buying Intel and AMD just because of the agentic CPU trend — [TradingKey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/261902902-agentic-ai-claude-anthropic-cpu-gpu-openclaw-nvidia-amd-sndk-tradingkey) (secondary).
- SK hynix posted a record Q1 profit as memory prices climbed — [CNBC, Apr 23 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/23/sk-hynix-earnings-ai-memory-shortage-hbm-demand.html). Its CEO said DRAM and HBM are effectively sold out through 2029, with the shortage lasting through 2030 — [Herald Business](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10917362).
- Korea launched **single-stock leveraged ETFs on May 27**, and retail investors bought a net ₩14T of them — [CNBC, Jul 20 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/20/give-me-my-money-back-south-korean-traders-leveraged-bets-unravel.html). This added retail leverage near the top.
- Situational Awareness LP's holdings reportedly grew to about $45B by June, and its return was +439% by June — [CNBC, Jul 31](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/31/leopold-aschenbrenner-situational-awareness-fund-fire-sale.html); [Forbes, Aug 17](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jimosman/2026/08/17/the-35-billion-ai-trading-loss-exposes-the-real-risk-of-leverage/). This points to large leveraged momentum money in the trade.

### Inferences
- The H1 rally broadened from Nvidia-centric GPU exposure to memory, equipment and CPUs. "Agents need more CPU and memory" was the main narrative link between agentic coding tools and semis. The most rigorous source (Axios) emphasizes the memory supply shortage rather than agents as such.
- The rally happened **despite** the Iran war oil shock (peak Brent about $118–126 in April) and CPI rising to about 4.2% in May. AI fundamentals dominated macro in H1.

### Gaps
- No Reuters, FT, WSJ or Bloomberg article was found that quantifies agentic coding tools' effect on token or compute demand, or that directly names Claude Code as a market driver. That link rests on analyst and commentary sources.
- No month-by-month SOX levels for January–May were found.

## Q3. Verifying the user's hypothesized sell-off drivers, plus other drivers

### Takeaway
**Verified:** the Iran war and oil shock (with caveats on timing), the inflation re-acceleration and Fed hikes, the Situational Awareness blow-up (July 2026), and the forced deleveraging of Korean retail investors (July, continuing into November). **Important additional drivers:** Samsung's earnings miss on July 7, HBM pricing worries, **AI data-center credit stress** (CoreWeave and Oracle CDS at records in Oct–Nov), the **10-year Treasury yield back above 5%**, **local voter rejection of data centers in the Nov 3 midterms**, weak guidance from Qualcomm and Arm, and **Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO on Nov 11–12**. Note that oil was near pre-war levels (~$73 Brent) when the SOX peaked in late June. Oil therefore looks more like a recurring and inflationary amplifier than the trigger of the July break.

### Cited Findings
**1. Iran war / oil shock: VERIFIED (it happened), but its role in the semis sell-off is mixed**
- The war began **Feb 28, 2026**. WTI rose from about $67 to a $111.54 peak on Apr 3, and Brent peaked at $118.03 on Apr 29 — [PLASTICS Industry Assn.](https://www.plasticsindustry.org/blog/oil-prices-fall-after-iran-ceasefire-will-u-s-inflation-finally-cool-down/). Another timeline puts Brent's high at $126.41 on Apr 30 — [Alupetra](https://www.alupetra.com/insights/iran-war-2026-timeline-oil-gas-markets.html) (conflicting peak figures).
- Oil spiked on Mar 9 as the war put the Strait of Hormuz into crisis — [Axios, Mar 9](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/09/oil-prices-iran-war-strait-hormuz). Brent stayed above $100 in mid-March — [Al Jazeera, Mar 13](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/economy/2026/3/13/oil-stays-above-100-a-barrel-amid-irans-stranglehold-on-strait-of-hormuz).
- A **ceasefire was announced Apr 8**, but Hormuz traffic stayed below pre-war levels — [Wikipedia: 2026 Iran war fuel crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war_fuel_crisis). One source reports a June 11 US strike as the most serious breakdown of the ceasefire; another reports a US–Iran MOU around June 17, with Brent below $80 on June 16 — [Ziromarket](https://www.ziromarket.com/blog/iran-us-ceasefire-oil-price-2026-global-impact); [Alupetra](https://www.alupetra.com/insights/iran-war-2026-timeline-oil-gas-markets.html) (unreconciled).
- Brent was **about $73 by June 29**, roughly its pre-war level, even as US and Iranian strikes threatened the reopening of Hormuz. Advantest fell 3.7% that day — [Al Jazeera, Jun 29](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/6/29/oil-prices-rise-as-us-iranian-strikes-threaten-strait-of-hormuz-reopening).
- On **Aug 31**, oil rose and stocks fell after the US hit Iranian sites in Hormuz. Brent swung between $72 and $102 within a month and stood at $90.58 — [Washington Times, Aug 31](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/aug/31/oil-prices-increase-stocks-fall-us-hits-iranian-sites-strait-hormuz/).
- In one tanker-attack episode, oil rose 5% and market losses were led by chip and tech stocks, with Samsung down 7% — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/markets/oil-prices-strait-hormuz-chip-stocks-rcna353328) (date not captured).
- Brent settled at **$95.84 on Friday, Nov 13**. Iranian claims of mines in Hormuz are unverified, and the IEA expects the market to return to surplus — [Admiral Markets, Nov 15](https://admiralmarkets.com/analytics/traders-blog/strait-of-hormuz-tanker-mines-oil-price) (broker commentary).
- **Verdict:** a "prolonged Iran war" is broadly accurate: the war started Feb 28, a ceasefire held only partly, and there were flare-ups in June and late August, with Hormuz disruption continuing into November. The oil shock was largest in March–April, during the rally. Oil had largely retraced by the late-June peak and then rebounded to the $90s from about August on. No source quantifies how much of the semis decline oil caused.

**2. Re-accelerating inflation and a hawkish Fed: VERIFIED**
- CPI was 2.4% YoY in February and 3.3% in March — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/inflation-cpi/news/540879); [CNBC, Apr 10](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/10/cpi-inflation-march-2026-breakdown.html). Headline CPI reached about 4.2% in May — [PLASTICS Industry Assn.](https://www.plasticsindustry.org/blog/oil-prices-fall-after-iran-ceasefire-will-u-s-inflation-finally-cool-down/).
- October CPI rose 0.4% m/m (forecast 0.3%; prior 0.6%) and core rose 0.3% m/m (forecast 0.2%). The "Fed turns hawkish as CPI beats" — [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/fed-turns-hawkish-as-cpi-beats-and-officials-back-further-rate-hikes-93CH-5026435). One source says **BLS could not publish most price indexes for October 2026, so official 12-month rates could not be calculated** for those categories — [Mundonow](https://www.mundonow.com/en/october-2026-inflation-rate/) (verify with BLS).
- The Cleveland Fed nowcast for November 2026: CPI 3.94% YoY, core CPI 2.87%, PCE 3.83%, core PCE 3.32% — [Cleveland Fed](https://www.clevelandfed.org/indicators-and-data/inflation-nowcasting).
- October PPI cooled month on month but hit a four-year high on an annual basis — [Babypips](https://www.babypips.com/news/headline-us-ppi-october-2026-producer-prices-annual-inflation-fed).

- Holdings fell from about **$45B to about $10B**, a "fire sale" — [CNBC, Jul 31](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/31/leopold-aschenbrenner-situational-awareness-fund-fire-sale.html). The Fortune piece of Aug 2 ("Wall Street's favorite bet comes undone as chips whipsaw market") falls in the same window — [Fortune, Aug 2](https://fortune.com/2026/08/02/wall-street-ai-trade-chip-stocks-volatility-sox-selloff/).

**4. Forced deleveraging of Korean retail/leveraged investors: VERIFIED (peak in mid-July; still running in November)**
- Korean retail investors bought a net ₩14T of single-stock leveraged ETFs after their May 27 launch. The KODEX SK Hynix 2x ETF fell about 70% from its June high (as of Jul 20). Regulators unveiled tougher rules for these products, and the Bank of Korea warned that retail leveraged investing was at a record — [CNBC, Jul 20](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/20/give-me-my-money-back-south-korean-traders-leveraged-bets-unravel.html).
- **SK hynix fell 15.37% on Jul 13**, its largest one-day drop in 18 years, with KOSPI more than 25% below its June peak. Goldman data cited by BigGo show more than 1.2M leveraged retail accounts hit margin calls by Jul 13, with about 320k–360k forcibly liquidated — [BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/ffef6d30-c6de-4ba3-9a3b-72cf55e29dea); [BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/1046f613-8e0d-4326-bd38-2d90a094e2cb). - The KODEX SK Hynix leveraged ETF fell 89.64% and the Samsung leveraged ETF 79.3% between June 22 and Nov 10. Retail investors are still buying leveraged products — [Korea JoongAng Daily, ~Nov 10–11](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/just-cant-quit-younbspkorean-retail-investors-still-placing-leveraged-bets-despite-near-wipeouts/12966125).

- **HBM pricing worry:** a Korean brokerage downgraded SK hynix over concerns about fixed-price HBM contracts — [24/7 Wall St., Jul 19](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/07/19/why-the-soxs-semiconductor-bear-etf-is-surging-as-chip-stocks-sell-off/?.tsrc=rss).
