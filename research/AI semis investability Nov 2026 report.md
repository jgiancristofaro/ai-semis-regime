# Buy AI chips in stages, not all at once

As of November 15, 2026, AI semiconductor equities look like a good investment on fundamentals and valuation but a poor one on timing, so the right move is a staged, time-based entry (four monthly tranches) led by TSM rather than an all-in buy of SOXX. The case for buying is strong. The SOX trades at about **13.7x forward earnings, a 22% discount to the S&P 500**, after a de-rating in which semiconductor earnings estimates *rose* by more than 70% this year. TSMC, AMD, Micron, SK hynix and ASML all raised guidance in their October–November reports and describe supply sold out into 2027–2029. Every trend and momentum signal argues for patience, though. SOXX is **31.6% below its June 22 peak**, our five-factor regime composite reads **0 of 5**, only **1 of 8** large AI chip names trades above its 200-day average, and a 50/200-day "death cross" is likely around December 8. A simple, rules-based system using textbook parameters would have bought in late December 2025, exited on **July 14, 2026**, and returned **+62.0% in 2026 versus +49.0% for buy-and-hold**, with less than half the drawdown. That system is still in cash. The agentic demand regime is real and probably larger than the 2025 "Claude Code moment," but it is constrained by supply, so it shows up first as pricing power for bottleneck suppliers like TSMC and HBM makers. The market is currently discounting that regime because of financing stress, 5% Treasury yields, a hiking Fed and Nvidia's sudden CEO change. Our recommendation: start a partial position now, concentrated in TSM; add after the November 17 Nvidia print and the December 8–9 FOMC if the November 5 lows hold; and commit fully only when SOXX reclaims its 200-day average (about $160) with the composite at 4 or better. Underweight memory, avoid leveraged ETFs, and hold NVDA pending earnings and clarity on CEO succession.

A note on sourcing: the price, drawdown, backtest and signal figures below come from the coordinator's own analysis of Yahoo Finance daily adjusted closes through November 13, 2026 ([Yahoo Finance SOXX history](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SOXX/history/)). Where the research notes conflict with that analysis, the primary-data figures are used and the conflict is flagged. Several valuation and positioning figures rest on a single secondary source; they are marked as such.

## An 81% stretch above trend flagged the top before July

The 2026 move was historic in both directions. The SOX made its record close of about **14,630 on June 22** after rising **87.8% in the three months to June 30**, its best quarter in records going back to 1994 ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/01/semiconductor-ai-stocks-chips)). It closed at **10,146 on November 13**, 30.7% below that peak and still up 43.2% for the year. SOXX, which split 3-for-1 effective November 5, peaked at a split-adjusted **$218.2** and closed at **$149.30**, up 49.0% YTD on a price basis ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SOXX/history/)). iShares reports +54.67% NAV total return as of November 12 ([iShares](https://www.ishares.com/us/products/239705/ishares-phlx-semiconductor-etf)), which is consistent once you account for the 3.67% drop on November 13. One research note estimated the SOX was "around 9,000–9,350" after November 5. That figure is wrong as a current level. BofA's reported **9,011 on November 5** ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/investing/52637)) fits the intraday or closing trough that day: SOXX fell 12.1% to $131.90 on November 5, about 39.5% below its peak. The index has since bounced roughly 12%.

The peaks were staggered, and that matters for anyone asking whether they could have "sold around June–July." The SOX, the Korean memory stocks and the KOSPI all peaked on June 22, driven by memory, equipment and Intel; Intel rose 216% in Q2 alone ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/01/semiconductor-ai-stocks-chips)). NVDA (record $239.2 on October 6) and TSM ($485.8 on October 5) peaked more than three months later. As of November 13, NVDA is **−31.9% from peak and −12.5% YTD**, TSM is **−17.9% from peak and +32.2% YTD**, Micron is +172.9% YTD despite a 35.8% drawdown, and SK hynix is **58.3% off its high** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TSM/history/)). One note puts TSM's drawdown at −15.7% from an October 2 high ([Heygotrade](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/news/tsmc-tsm-stock-news-november-13-2026/)); the primary-data figure of −17.9% from October 5 is used here.

### The rules that worked, and how honest the result is

We tested five signals using standard parameters, not ones tuned to 2026: a 200-day trend filter; a 50/200-day moving-average cross; monthly dual momentum; a five-factor regime composite; and the composite plus a "blow-off exit" overlay. Each was tested on SOXX with a one-day signal lag, 5bp of cost per side, and 0% on cash. The composite holds a full position at a score of 4–5, half at 3, and goes flat at 2 or below. Its five factors are: price above the 200-day SMA; positive 63-day momentum; SOXX/SPY relative strength above its 100-day mean; 20-day realized volatility below 1.5x its one-year median; and a 63-day rise in the 10-year yield of less than 50bp. The overlay exits when SOXX's percentage extension above its 200-day SMA has exceeded its expanding, past-only 95th percentile within the last 30 days *and* price closes below the 50-day SMA. It re-enters only at a 5/5 composite with price above the 50-day.

| Strategy | 2026 YTD | 2026 max DD | 2002–25 CAGR | 2002–25 Sharpe | 2002–25 max DD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buy & hold | +49.0% | −39.6% | 14.3% | 0.58 | −66.8% |
| 200-day trend | +56.8% | −29.0% | 9.6% | 0.54 | −42.2% |
| 50/200 cross | +49.0% | −39.6% | 13.1% | 0.67 | −34.2% |
| Dual momentum (monthly) | +49.0% | −39.6% | 7.5% | 0.45 | −49.1% |
| Regime composite | +55.0% | −28.8% | 7.9% | 0.47 | −42.6% |
| **Composite + blow-off exit** | **+62.0%** | **−17.6%** | 8.0% | 0.49 | −42.6% |

*Source: coordinator backtest on Yahoo Finance daily adjusted closes aligned to the US trading calendar, data through Nov 13, 2026. Figures were corrected on Nov 15 after a calendar-alignment fix (see the interactive page, section 03, for the regime signal definitions).*

Here is how the winning rule played out in 2026. It went long on **December 22, 2025**. It stepped aside from **March 6 to April 7** during the Iran-war oil shock: the war began February 28, and WTI peaked at $111.54 on April 3 ([PLASTICS Industry Assn.](https://www.plasticsindustry.org/blog/oil-prices-fall-after-iran-ceasefire-will-u-s-inflation-finally-cool-down/)). It re-entered as the ceasefire took hold and rode the parabola. It exited on **July 14**, the day after Samsung's earnings disappointment triggered a sector sell-off ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/07/chip-stocks-ai-selloff-samsung.html)) and five days before SK hynix's 15% single-day crash ([BigGo](https://finance.biggo.com/news/ffef6d30-c6de-4ba3-9a3b-72cf55e29dea)). It has stayed out since. The result is robust within its family. All 27 variants in a grid beat 2026 buy-and-hold, returning +52% to +76% with maximum drawdowns of −12% to −29%; the grid crossed three base strategies, three extension thresholds (90th/95th/97.5th percentile) and three exit averages (21/50/100-day). The 20-day exit sold on June 8, two weeks *before* the peak, and still won. The 50-day exit sold July 14, and the 100-day exit sold July 30. Every variant was out before August.

The signal that mattered most was extension. On June 22, SOXX traded **81% above its 200-day average**. The previous maximum since 2001 was +44%, in 2003. Several contemporaneous "froth" markers confirmed it. Long semiconductors ranked first as the BofA Fund Manager Survey's most crowded trade from June onward ([KuCoin, secondary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bofa-survey-53-of-fund-managers-view-longing-semiconductors-as-the-most-crowded-trade)). Korea launched single-stock leveraged ETFs on May 27 and retail investors poured ₩14T into them ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/20/give-me-my-money-back-south-korean-traders-leveraged-bets-unravel.html)). Korean margin loans hit a record ₩38T in early June ([Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/06/01/koreas-margin-loans-hit-record-38-trillion-won-as-kospi)). SMH's trailing P/E reached about 48.5x ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4964031-dont-waste-this-tech-crash)). Situational Awareness, a roughly 4x-levered long-AI-infrastructure fund that was up 439% by June, was liquidated in late July ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/31/leopold-aschenbrenner-situational-awareness-fund-fire-sale.html)). The fund's collapse amplified the break rather than causing it, since the SOX was already in a bear market by about July 17.

Two caveats keep this from being a victory lap. First, from 2002 to 2025 **no timing rule beat buy-and-hold on total return**. The blow-off overlay compounded at 8.0% against 13.8%. The 50/200 cross delivered the best risk-adjusted profile, with a 0.66 Sharpe and a −34% maximum drawdown versus −67%. Timing rules buy drawdown control, not extra return. Second, the overlay is a tail-event tool, and it was designed with knowledge of what a parabola looks like. Its 2026 result is credible because textbook parameters worked across the whole grid, but it should not be sold as a reliable alpha source. Its practical value is the discipline it imposes now: it says stay out until trend and volatility normalize.

## Momentum has flipped from tailwind to "correction"

SOXX is no longer a momentum trade in the short run, though its long-horizon momentum remains positive. As of November 13, the regime composite scores **1/5**; only the rates check passes. The 200-day trend filter has been flat since November 2, after a whipsaw from October 23 to 26. Twenty-day realized volatility is **77%, against a one-year median of 39%**. The 10-year Treasury yield is **5.15%**, up 43bp in 63 trading days, after a 2026 high of 5.31% on October 5. SOXX sits below its 20-day ($156.8), 200-day ($160.3) and 50-day ($171.9) averages ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SOXX/history/)). The 50/200 cross is technically still long, with the 50-day 7.2% above the 200-day, but a death cross arrives around **December 8** if price stays flat. Breadth is poor: of eight AI semis, only AMD is above its 200-day and only one is above its 50-day.

The Goulding–Harvey–Mazzoleni "turning points" framework, which compares 12-month slow momentum with 1-month fast momentum, has classified SOXX as a **"Correction"** (slow positive, fast negative) in both October and November. Twelve-minus-one-month momentum is still **+72%**, while three-month momentum is **−20%**. Historically this is the weakest of the four states for semis, but it is not a disaster.

| Momentum state (SOX, monthly, 2001–2025) | n | Avg 3m fwd | Avg 12m fwd |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bull | 117 | +3.9% | +18.6% |
| **Correction (current)** | 71 | +2.9% | **+13.6%** |
| Bear | 60 | +4.9% | +18.4% |
| Rebound | 52 | +2.4% | +18.8% |

The 3-month hit rate in the Correction state is 58%. The best conditional evidence comes from 15 de-clustered episodes since 2000 in which the SOX was at least 28% below its 52-week high and below its 200-day. They averaged **+18.1% over the next 12 months, with a 60% hit rate**, against an unconditional +14.6%. The distribution is bimodal. In 2001–02 and 2008 the index kept falling, while after March 2020, October 2022 and April 2025 it rallied 50–114% within a year. 2026 has one feature none of those episodes shared: twelve-month momentum was still +44% at the signal date. Almost every prior deep drawdown arrived with negative 12-month momentum. Two readings follow, and both are plausible. Either this is an unusually violent mid-cycle reset in a long-term uptrend, or it is the early phase of a cycle top in which the slow signal has not yet caught up.

The asset's behavior has changed too. In the second half of 2026, SOXX's daily beta to SPY rose to **2.98** (from 1.78 in 2025) and its correlation with the KOSPI tripled to **0.42**. SPY is only 3.2% below its peak, while SOXX is down 31.6%. Semis have become a high-beta, deleveraging-driven asset, with Korean memory margin calls feeding through to US chip prices. That is why trend rules matter more than usual right now: price is reacting to positioning, not to earnings.

## The agentic demand step-change is real, but supply caps it

The case for a new "Claude Code moment" is stronger on the evidence than skeptics allow. Its effect on chip stocks, however, comes through pricing more than volume. Usage data from the largest platforms shows a step-change. Google reported processing about **3.2 quadrillion tokens a month in May 2026, roughly 7x a year earlier** ([GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260520-google-monthly-tokens-processed/)). Anthropic told investors its annualized revenue run-rate reached **about $65B at end-July 2026**, up from about $9B at end-2025 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/17/anthropic-says-annualized-revenue-climbed-to-65-billion-in-july.html)). Run-rate is not recognized revenue, but that sevenfold climb dwarfs the late-2025 Claude Code step, when Claude Code's own run-rate went from about $1B to $2.5B ([Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/claude-code-25-billion-arr-anthropic-2026/)). Agentic workloads are token-hungry: Anthropic's engineering team measured single agents at about 4x the tokens of a chat turn and multi-agent orchestration at about 15x ([getnadir, citing Anthropic](https://getnadir.com/blog/multi-agent-orchestration-15x-token-cost/)). A shift in usage mix toward agents therefore multiplies compute demand even with flat user counts. Google's token figure includes consumer product traffic, and the Microsoft figures are secondary, so treat the cross-company comparisons as directional.

The spread of agents to non-technical work is early but visible. OpenAI's own telemetry shows weekly active enterprise Codex users in Legal growing about **108x from February to June 2026, versus about 5x in Engineering**. Legal, Finance and Recruiting made it their primary AI tool around April ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/how-agents-are-transforming-work/)). That is self-selected vendor data from a low base. Broader surveys show orchestrated multi-agent systems at scale in only about **15–18% of large organizations** ([Joget, summarizing KPMG and Deloitte](https://joget.com/ai-agent-adoption-in-2026-what-the-analysts-data-shows/)). The pattern resembles coding in early 2025: hypergrowth at the frontier, with most of the token volume still ahead, probably in 2027–2028.

Recursive self-improvement now has its first credible measurement. METR's November 5 independent assessment of Anthropic found interviews and surveys "consistent with AI usage causing a doubling in the pace of capabilities progress," mostly through AI assistance rather than autonomous research ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/)). METR's Time Horizon 1.1 puts the doubling time of agent task length at about 89 days since 2024 ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-01-29-time-horizon-1-1/)), but METR also warns that longer horizons do not translate linearly into automation ([METR](https://metr.org/notes/2026-01-22-time-horizon-limitations/)). The finding covers one lab and rests on interviews, so a 2027 "takeoff" remains speculation. The investment implication is narrower and firmer: a lab that gets 2x research throughput from AI runs more internal agents and more experiments, and so buys more compute.

The constraint is supply. SK hynix says its DRAM and HBM are effectively **sold out through 2029** under 14 long-term agreements ([Sammyfans](https://www.sammyfans.com/2026/10/28/sk-hynix-q3-margin-hits-record-80-secures-14-ltas-with-memory-supply-booked-to-2029/amp/)). ASML says planned 2027–2028 supply "will not fully meet the demand of our customers" ([investinglive](https://investinglive.com/stocks/asml-says-2027-and-2028-supply-will-fall-short-of-demand-as-q3-sales-hit-a-record/)). Nvidia put its revenue opportunity per gigawatt at about **$25B for Blackwell and $40B for Vera Rubin** ([NVDA Q2 FY27 transcript](https://s201.q4cdn.com/141608511/files/content_files/TRANSCRIPT_-NVIDIA-Corp-NVDA-US-Q2-2027-Earnings-Call-26-August-2026-5_00-PM-ET.pdf)), so dollar silicon content rises about 60% even at a constant build rate. Because 2027 HBM and CoWoS capacity is booked, any further demand surprise in 2027 shows up as **price** at the bottleneck layers: leading-edge foundry, advanced packaging, HBM and lithography. Nikkei reported TSMC planning 5–10% base price increases for 2027 ([The Standard/Nikkei](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/finance/article/337799/TSMC-to-raise-chipmaking-prices-by-up-to-10-percent-in-2027-Nikkei-Asia-reports)); TSMC declined to confirm. That points straight at TSM as the cleanest expression of a demand regime the market is not currently paying for.

The counter-evidence deserves weight. Token prices fell below $1 per million in August 2026 ([BigGo, secondary](https://finance.biggo.com/news/8a9ab26e-9958-41db-92b7-51645a29ca08)). In one survey, 79% of companies running production AI said their GPUs sit idle at least half the time ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/infrastructure/ai-bubble-update-79-of-companies-say-their-gpus-sit-idle-at-least-half-the-time-yet-most-plan-new-ai-platforms)). Writer's survey found only 29% of organizations report significant ROI ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/petercohan/2026/06/11/the-ai-bubble-isnt-bursting-but-a-vicious-price-war-is-here/)). So far Jevons-style elasticity dominates, with volumes growing far faster than prices fall. The vulnerable buyers are enterprises and leveraged neoclouds, not the capacity-short frontier labs and hyperscalers.

That proviso is the whole bear case.

## Financing stress, not demand, is the bear case to fear

The most dangerous risk is a break in the financing loop that funds marginal GPU demand, not a collapse in end demand. CoreWeave bonds yielded more than **16%** and its CDS topped **1,100–1,289bp** in early November, while Oracle CDS reached about 298bp ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/coreweave-bond-yield-tops-16percent-as-ai-credit-stress-rises.html)). The IMF's October GFSR named an AI-driven correction one of three top near-term stability risks. It counted 14 circular deals since 2023 that channeled about **$300B of equity and $650B of debt** to weaker AI firms against $1.4T of revenue commitments ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328970/20261014/imf-names-14t-ai-financing-loop-top-trigger-global-market-shock.htm)). The IMF document itself was not accessed. Nvidia had acted as a lender of last resort, including a backstop of up to $125B on a $500B credit program ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/11/nvidia-jensen-huang-ai-financial-system-risk.html)); those terms have not been verified against a filing. That role is now in question. **Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO**, per an SEC 8-K dated November 12, with CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO and Harvey Jones as board chair ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000082/nvidiaannouncesceotransiti.htm); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo-live-updates.html)). The board had opened an independent investigation on November 9 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/nvidia-jensen-huang-chinese-spy-investigation.html)). One valuation note written before the 8-K says Huang "remains CEO"; that is superseded. CreditSights expects circular financing to moderate after his exit ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/13/nvidia-jensen-huang-resignation-ai-financing.html)), which is bearish for neocloud demand at the margin.

Macro and politics compound the financing risk. The Fed hiked 25bp to 3.75–4.00% on September 16, its first hike since 2023 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-rate-decision-september-2026.html)). Two members dissented in favor of another hike on October 28 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/28/fed-meeting-today-live-updates.html)), and markets now price a hike on December 8–9. The Cleveland Fed's November nowcast puts CPI at 3.94% ([Cleveland Fed](https://www.clevelandfed.org/indicators-and-data/inflation-nowcasting)). Brent is near $96 and Hormuz disruption continues ([Admiral Markets](https://admiralmarkets.com/analytics/traders-blog/strait-of-hormuz-tanker-mines-oil-price)). In the November 3 midterms, local voters restricted data centers in **45 of 47 ballot measures** ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/2026-election-results-show-widespread-opposition-to-ai-data-centers/)). ## Recommendation: a four-tranche, time-based entry led by TSM

The coordinator's final entry backtest changed the plan from "one-third now, the rest on trend confirmation" to a time-based schedule. It covered 15 prior episodes since 2000 in which the SOX was at least 28% below its 52-week high and below its 200-day average, and compared four entry plans over the next 12 months:

| Entry plan | Mean 12m | Median 12m | Median worst drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|
| All-in at signal | +21.4% | +17.9% | −19.4% |
| **4 equal monthly tranches** | **+17.0%** | **+20.4%** | **−8.5%** |
| ⅓ now, ⅔ on 200-day reclaim | +13.4% | +9.9% | −13.4% |
| Wait for 200-day reclaim | +9.5% | +6.1% | n/a |

Waiting for the 200-day reclaim gave up about half of the average rebound. It did not avoid the 2001, 2002 or 2008 losses either, because those reclaims proved false. Trend rules worked as an *exit* in 2026 but are a poor *re-entry* tool. Monthly tranches had the highest median return and less than half the median drawdown of a lump sum. (Source: coordinator analysis, `analysis/staged.py`, Yahoo Finance data.)

**Schedule.** Build the target position in four equal tranches: the week of November 17 (after Nvidia's print), about December 15 (after the FOMC), about January 15 (after TSMC's Q4 and 2027 guidance) and about February 15 (after the hyperscaler capex calls). Split it roughly **40% TSM, 45% SOXX and 15% NVDA**, with the NVDA sleeve started only after November 17 and once there is clarity on the CEO search. TSM is the core holding. It has the strongest relative performance (−17.9% from peak against −31.9% for NVDA), proven pricing power, guidance raised to about 44% growth with October revenue up 59.7%, rising price targets, and no governance overhang. Its current forward P/E and ADR premium were not verified. **Accelerate** the remaining tranches if SOXX closes above its 200-day average (about $160) with the regime composite at 4 or more.

**Invalidation: stop adding if any of these occur.**
- SOXX closes below its November 5 low of $131.90 while CoreWeave or Oracle credit widens further.
- A top-five hyperscaler cuts 2027 capex in the January–February calls.
- TSMC monthly revenue growth falls below +20% year on year.
- Consensus SOX forward EPS is cut by more than 10% in a quarter.
- The 10-year yield rises above 5.5%.

These are the 2000–02 and 2008 signatures. In those episodes earnings collapsed, which is what separated the losing drawdown episodes from the winning ones.

Treat individual names differently. **Underweight memory** (MU, SK hynix, Samsung) despite the strongest current earnings. A 4x multiple says the market is pricing a cycle peak, and the Korea leverage channel (0.42 correlation) makes these names the transmission belt for forced selling. **Hold NVDA** without adding before November 17 and before a permanent CEO is named, since the Street is split between neutral targets of $186–200 and buy targets of $250–350 ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/analyst-stock-ratings/price-target/26/11/63216627/nvidia-price-targets-cut-evercore-apple-jensen-huang)). **Avoid leveraged ETFs** entirely: 20-day volatility of 77% makes their volatility decay brutal, as SOXL's −49% month against roughly −30% for the index shows. The near-term catalysts, in order, are: Nvidia earnings on November 17; Huang's closed-door congressional briefing and the November BofA FMS around November 18; the likely death cross around December 8; the FOMC on December 8–9; Broadcom's FQ4 report in mid-December; TSMC's November revenue around December 10 (inferred from its monthly reporting pattern); and hyperscaler 2027 capex guidance in January–February 2027.

## Conclusion

The two halves of 2026 teach different lessons. The first half shows that price extension, not valuation, flagged the top. Semis looked "reasonable" at 26x forward even as they traded 81% above trend, and the extension signal, not the P/E, got investors out. The second half shows the reverse: at 13.7x on rising estimates, valuation now argues for buying while trend argues for waiting. Investors who trusted only the multiple would have bought too early in July and again in October. Investors who trusted only trend will probably buy 10–20% above the eventual low. Combining the two, with valuation deciding *whether* to own the group and trend deciding *how much*, is the only approach that respects both the strength of the fundamentals and the two-way history of deep semiconductor drawdowns.

The agentic regime changes *which* chips to own more than *whether* to own chips. In a world where demand outruns sold-out supply, gains accrue to the bottleneck holders with pricing power, and TSMC is the cleanest of these. Memory makers capture the same scarcity but face a visible 2028 supply wave and a leveraged retail shareholder base. The question that will decide the next 12 months is not whether AI demand is real but whether the debt financing behind its marginal buyers holds while the Fed is still tightening. Watch CoreWeave spreads and January's capex guides more closely than token counts.
