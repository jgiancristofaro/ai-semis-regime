2026: a melt-up led by memory, not Nvidia

Your read on the year holds, with one correction to the narrative. The rally was not an Nvidia story. NVDA is −12.5% YTD, while Micron is +173%, AMD +149% and SK hynix +87% (after being +300% at the June peak). The SOX index posted +87.8% in Q2, its best quarter since 1994 [1]. It was a memory-and-everything-agentic squeeze. Memory prices, CPU demand for agents and HBM shortages did the lifting [2].

The backtest: buy in January, get out in July

We tested five pre-specified rules on SOXX using textbook parameters, none tuned on 2026. The one that fits your narrative is the Regime Composite + Blow-off Exit. Its regime score has five parts: price above the 200-day, 3-month momentum, strength versus the S&P, calm volatility, and the 10-year not up more than 50bp in three months. On top of that sits one overextension rule: if SOXX has been stretched beyond the 95th percentile of its own history above the 200-day and then closes below its 50-day, exit. Re-enter only when all five regime signals agree. Section 03 explains each signal and what it takes to flip it.

Caveat. From 2002 to 2025 no timing rule beat buy-and-hold on total return. Their value was cutting drawdowns: the 50/200 cross took max drawdown from −67% to −34% and had the best Sharpe. The blow-off exit is a tail-event tool. It paid off in 2026 because the stretch was nearly twice anything seen since 2001, and in ordinary years it costs you.

The regime model: what the score measures and what flips it

At every close the model asks five yes/no questions about SOXX. The score is the number of "yes" answers. The rules are fixed and use standard settings, not values fitted to 2026:

How the score sets the position

ScoreRegime compositeWith blow-off exit
5 / 5Fully longFully long. After a blow-off exit, re-entry needs 5/5 and a close above the 50-day.
4 / 5Fully longFully long, unless a blow-off exit is active
3 / 5Half positionHalf, unless a blow-off exit is active
0–2 / 5Flat (cash)Flat

Signals are read at the close and acted on at the next close. The blow-off exit fires when SOXX has been stretched above the 95th percentile of its own history versus the 200-day within the last 30 days, then closes below the 50-day.

Daily correlations show the market regime flipping. In H1, rising yields and oil hurt semis. In H2, semis' beta to the S&P rose to ~3x, and their correlation with Korea's KOSPI more than tripled, from 0.13 to 0.44. In H2 semis traded as a leveraged, crowded position being unwound. Credit check: is the AI credit stress spreading?

CoreWeave and Oracle credit came under real pressure this fall. The question for a broader sell-off is whether that stress spreads into the general bond market, as it did in 2008. So far it hasn't. Broad credit spreads are near the tightest levels on record, and the pressure on stocks is coming from rising real yields instead.

Spread over Treasuries, percentage points. FRED: Moody's Baa minus 10-yr Treasury (BAA10Y); ICE BofA US High Yield OAS (BAMLH0A0HYM2; FRED carries only the last 3 years). Chicago Fed financial conditions index (NFCI): ; below 0 means looser than average.

Would a credit signal have improved the regime model?

We tested the Baa spread two ways, with no tuning on 2026: as a veto that halves the position when credit is stressed, and as a sixth vote in the score. "Stressed" means the spread widened more than 0.30 or 0.50 points over 63 sessions.

Verdict: credit never flagged stress in 2026, so it wouldn't have changed the July exit. Over 2002–2025 it helped modestly and consistently; the gains show up in the 2007–09 and 2020–25 windows. We keep the five-signal model and track credit as a kill switch: stop adding if the Baa spread widens more than 0.30 points in 3 months. It is currently over that window.

Agent swarms beyond engineering: early, real

  • OpenAI telemetry: weekly enterprise Codex users in Legal grew ~108x Feb→Jun 2026, vs ~5x in Engineering. Legal, Finance and Recruiting made it their primary tool around April. This is vendor data from a low base [15].
  • Anthropic's Cowork ("Claude Code for the rest of your work") went GA on Apr 9, 2026.
  • Only ~18% of large firms (KPMG) run multi-agent systems at scale, so most of the non-technical wave is still ahead.

Our read: this is already happening, and it is the bigger demand lever of the two because it multiplies seats × tasks × agents.

Recursive self-improvement: credible, but early

  • METR's first external assessment found interview and survey evidence consistent with ~2x faster capability progress at Anthropic, mostly from AI assisting researchers [13].
  • METR's task-horizon measure now doubles about every 131 days (89 days since 2024). METR warns that this does not translate linearly into automation [16].
  • Hassabis says all leading labs are focused on RSI. Altman targets an automated AI researcher by March 2028. AI chip-design agents are only partially autonomous so far.

Our read: RSI is a 2027–28 upside option. It can also cut either way, because efficiency gains lower compute per task.

Valuation: the sell-off was in the multiple, not the earnings

From the June peak, the SOX forward multiple roughly halved, from about 26x to about 13.7x. That is a 22% discount to the S&P 500 at 17.7x and close to the ~12x trough level of past cycles [7]. Over the same period forward earnings estimates rose: SOX forward EPS is up about 113% YTD (Bernstein, via secondary) and up 70%+ from January to July (Natixis). Every major semi that reported since June raised its outlook [7].

08

How to buy a broken leader: the entry backtest

We found 15 prior episodes since 2000 when the SOX was at least 28% below its 52-week high and below its 200-day average, as it is now. For each one we compared four entry plans over the next 12 months. Waiting for the trend to repair (a reclaim of the 200-day) gave up about half of the rebound and did not avoid the 2001 and 2008 losses, because the reclaims were false. Buying in four monthly tranches had the best risk/reward: the highest median return and less than half the median drawdown of buying everything at once.

What separated good from bad episodes: the losers (2001, 2002, 2008) came with collapsing earnings. The winners (2009, 2020, Oct 2022, Apr 2025) came while estimates held. Today estimates are rising, and 12-month momentum is still +44%, a combination with almost no precedent. That is why the kill criteria below are tied to estimates and capex, not price.

Risks, kill criteria and the calendar

Financing

AI credit is the weak link. The IMF counts 14 circular deals (~$300B equity, ~$650B debt) against $1.4T of revenue commitments, and ~$600B of AI debt was issued in 2026 [5].

Rates

10-year at 5.15%, the Fed hiking, CPI ~4%. In H2 a 100bp yield rise mapped to a meaningfully larger semis drawdown than in 2025.

Positioning

Retail is still buying the dip: SOXL took ~$7B in one October week. Capitulation looks incomplete, which supports BofA's call for a Q1-2027 trough [9].

Memory cycle

New fabs ramp in 2H27. Memory is 68% of major cloud providers' 2027 capex (TrendForce), which squeezes budgets for compute [5].

Recommendation

Yes, AI semis are investable today, but not as a lump sum and not as the momentum trade they were. The early-2026 setup was trend plus momentum. The current one is value plus fundamentals against a broken tape. That calls for a different playbook: buy on a schedule, prefer quality, and use fundamental kill switches instead of price stops.

SOXX45%Same four-tranche schedule. Accelerate the remaining tranches if the regime score reaches 4/5 (section 03 shows the price that takes; about at the next close today, falling as the window rolls).
NVDA15%Wait for Nov 17 results and clarity on the CEO search. The governance discount may be the opportunity, but the event risk is binary.

What would make us more aggressive: an NVDA beat-and-raise that holds, a BofA survey showing semis no longer crowded, a December Fed pause, and a regime score of 4/5 or better. Under the rules that worked in 2026, those together are the "all clear".
